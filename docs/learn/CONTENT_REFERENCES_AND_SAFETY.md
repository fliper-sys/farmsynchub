# Content references and safety guide

Content is educational and must be adapted to crop, variety, species, production system, local climate, buyer requirements and current Nigerian/state rules. It avoids universal pesticide rates and veterinary drug dosages.

- FAO, Prevention and control of mycotoxins in stored grains and seeds: prompt drying, dry storage and moisture control. https://www.fao.org/4/x5008e/X5008e01.htm
- Ghana Ministry of Food and Agriculture, Aflatoxin Contamination in Groundnut and Maize: field and postharvest risk reduction. https://www.mofa.gov.gh/site/images/pdf/production_guides/Aflatoxin_Contamination_In_Groundnut_And_Maize.pdf
- WorldFish (2024), Smallholder tilapia farming in Nigeria: Guidelines of best biosecurity practices. https://digitalarchive.worldfishcenter.org/items/6e1e7e4a-2f40-4e09-92c8-44264d207e15
- WorldFish (2023), Better management practices for fish production systems in Delta and Ogun States, Nigeria. https://digitalarchive.worldfishcenter.org/items/d9c42989-6931-4788-8ff0-fe76f6485643
- Postharvest Education Foundation, Reducing Postharvest Losses in Sub-Saharan Africa. https://postharvest.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PEF_2023_Manual_1_RPL_SSA.pdf

Always verify pesticide registration and labels, veterinary product instructions and withdrawal periods, food-processing validation, equipment manuals, water quality, land rights, labour, tax and environmental rules with the relevant Nigerian/state authority or qualified professional. Cassava processing must follow a recognised validated process to reduce cyanogenic compounds. Visual checks do not establish that grain or feed is aflatoxin-free.
